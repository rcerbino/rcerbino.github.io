Differential dynamic microscopy

DDM, hands on

Differential dynamic microscopy (DDM) turns an ordinary microscope movie into a light scattering experiment: it measures how fast the patterns of each size in the image fade, without following any single particle or cell. From those rates follow sizes, speeds and much more. Each page below simulates an experiment in your browser and takes you through the whole analysis, from the raw movie to the result.

About these pages

These pages are a divertissement. For fun, we took configurations close to those of our SoMeX lab and used them to simulate some typical DDM experiments in your browser. The simulations are simple and rely on many approximations; each page says which. More applications will follow. Found something wrong or not working? Please let us (me and Anthropic Claude) know through the contact page.

If you like them, The hitchhiker's guide to differential dynamic microscopy (Lattuada et al., J. Chem. Phys. 163, 161501, 2025) explains how DDM works in practice, and fastDDM is our open-source code to analyse your own movies.
