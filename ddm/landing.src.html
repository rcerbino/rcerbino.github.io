Differential dynamic microscopy

DDM, hands on

Differential dynamic microscopy (DDM), introduced in 2008 (R. Cerbino and V. Trappe, Phys. Rev. Lett. 100, 188102), turns an ordinary microscope movie into a light scattering experiment: it measures how fast the patterns of each size in the image fade, without following any single particle or cell. From those rates follow sizes, speeds and much more. Each page below simulates an experiment in your browser and takes you through the whole analysis, from the raw movie to the result.

New here? Try doubling the bead size: predict how the curves move, then watch the page check it.

Brownian motion

How big are they?

Bright field, colloidal beads (sketch)

Size colloidal particles from how fast they wander, as in dynamic light scattering, but with a microscope and in samples where light scattering struggles.

Spheres, ellipsoids and rods, some of which turn as well as wander; seven imaging modes from bright field to confocal; cameras, noise, depth of field and what each does to the result.

Active motion

How fast do they swim?

Phase contrast, bacteria (sketch)

Measure how fast bacteria, algae or sperm swim, how much the speeds spread and which share of the cells swims, from a motility movie with thousands of cells.

E. coli strains, Salmonella, B. subtilis, Chlamydomonas, Euglena and bull sperm, set up as in published experiments, or cells of your own design.

  • Swimming speed
  • Speed spread

Drift

Where are they going?

Bright field, beads in a flow (sketch)

Measure the speed and direction of particles carried by a flow, by gravity or by a temperature gradient, while they keep jiggling, and find out how slow a drift a movie can still show.

Plug and channel flows; sedimentation with the microscope upright or on its side; thermophoresis of polystyrene beads with Soret coefficients from published measurements.

  • Flow
  • Sedimentation
  • Thermophoresis

Viscoelasticity

Liquid or solid?

Bright field, beads in a gel (sketch)

Measure how elastic and how viscous a soft material is, from how far tracer beads mixed into it get: microrheology with a microscope, from a drop of sample.

Simple liquids, wormlike micelles, polymer solutions and a gel forming, with moduli known exactly; from the movie to G' and G'' over several decades of frequency.

  • Microrheology
  • G' and G''
  • Gelation

About these pages

These pages are a divertissement. For fun, we took configurations close to those of our SoMeX lab and used them to simulate some typical DDM experiments in your browser. The simulations are simple and rely on many approximations; each page says which. More applications will follow. Found something wrong or not working? Please let us (me and Anthropic Claude) know through the contact page.
