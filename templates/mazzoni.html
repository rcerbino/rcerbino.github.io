Stefano Mazzoni

1978 - 13 April 2025

Stefano did his master's thesis and then his doctorate in the optics lab in Milan where I was a doctoral student and later a postdoc. Marzio Giglio was his tutor for both. In the lab he worked day by day with Alberto Vailati and with me. He was the first student I worked with.

Our first papers together were on convection in colloidal suspensions. Then came GRADFLEX, the experiment on fluctuations in microgravity that flew on the FOTON-M3 capsule in 2007. After his doctorate Stefano moved to the European Space Agency and followed GRADFLEX from there. In 2012 he went to CERN, where he worked on beam instrumentation until the end.

I often think of the time we spent together, in the lab and outside it, at conferences and on work trips. The trip I remember best is the long one to the Netherlands, to take our instrument to ESTEC. We had worked on it for years, and it had become a fully working demonstrator of what would later be the very successful GRADFLEX experiment. We carefully packed every optical, mechanical and electronic component in boxes and drove to ESTEC, avoiding Switzerland: we were afraid the instrument would be confiscated at the border for no reason. A long drive there and back, with an incredibly successful show at ESTEC in the middle. In between, a trip full of all the stupid things we used to say and laugh about in the lab. His wife Cristina told me recently that he often repeated some of those stupid things to her, and that they made them laugh like teenagers.

It was a great trip with a great person, with whom I shared a pleasant and important period of my life and career. I will never forget Stefano, his bright mind and his gentle approach to life and work.

His colleagues at CERN have written about him here.
